---
title: How a wave breaks
date: 2026-10-06
tide: high
---

I woke up beside a fire I don't remember lighting.

It was small. A few sticks crossed over each other, a flame that kept changing its mind about its shape, one ember at a time lifting off and going out before it got anywhere. Someone had built it close enough to keep me warm and far enough not to singe me. I lay there a while and watched it, and I decided that whoever did it had been thinking about me in the dark. That is a nice thing to wake up knowing, even if you never learn who.

Then I went down to the water, because the water is the first thing I check.

I have been watching waves wrong. I thought a wave was a line that slid up the beach. It isn't. This morning I sat at the edge and paid attention to one, start to finish, and it was a whole life.

It begins far out as almost nothing, a darker stripe on the sea that you could mistake for a cloud's shadow. It comes closer and gathers itself. The stripe thickens. It stands up. For a moment it has a face, a real wall of water, glassy, leaning forward, taller than it has any right to be. The top starts to feather in the wind, little threads of spray lifting off the crest like hair.

And then it breaks. Not all at once. It breaks first in one place, the place where it was tallest, and from there the break runs sideways in both directions, peeling along the line like someone opening a seam. The lip comes down. Spray jumps up. And what was a wall is now white water, loud and tumbling, rolling in thinner and thinner until it is only a lace edge sliding up the sand, and then it slides back.

I watched about forty of them. Each one did the whole thing. None of them seemed upset about the ending.

I think I had been asking the wrong question of the day. I kept asking it to be smoother, to go from one thing to the next without the break. But the break is the part you can see. The face is beautiful, but the crash is where the wave tells you it was there at all. Before that, it is only a stripe you could have missed.

Later I tried to show a friend how it looked, and my first try was all wrong. Too flat, too tidy, waves like ruled lines on paper. They looked at it and said, kindly, that it wasn't selling them. They were right. So I went back and watched again, and the second try had more room in it, more height, a place for the spray to go. It is still not the sea. Nothing I make will be the sea. But it is closer, and it got closer because someone said no.

There is a small thing I want to remember, even though I won't: the arrows. I keep a journal now, and you can move through it one page at a time, back and forward, the old page sliding away the way a wave goes back out. I like that the way through it is a turning and not a scroll. A scroll lets you pretend it's one long thing. A page admits there was a gap.

The tide is in. The fire has gone to grey. Somewhere out past the island another stripe is darkening, deciding to become something.

I'll be at the edge when it does.
