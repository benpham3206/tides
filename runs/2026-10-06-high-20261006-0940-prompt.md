# FABLE Judge Prompt

You are judging a draft against the rubric below. Be strict, concrete, and useful.
Use only the supplied draft. Do not assume context outside the fenced text.

## Eligibility gate (check before scoring)
- The draft must be prose intended for a reader: sentences and paragraphs, not capture. If the draft is mostly fragments, bare lists, headings without bodies, or pasted tool output, REFUSE to score. Reply only: "INELIGIBLE: raw capture, not prose. Score it after it has been written for a reader."
- The draft must be one document, not a concatenation of unrelated notes. If it is a multi-document dump, REFUSE with: "INELIGIBLE: multiple documents. Submit one."

## Rubric Checklist
1. [known_new] Known-New Contract (weight: 3)
   Test: Sample five sentences at random. For each, does its opening word-cluster stand on ground the previous sentence built? Quote any sentence that asks the reader to stand on unbuilt ground.
2. [no_restatement] Never Restate (weight: 2)
   Test: Find any sentence that could be deleted with zero information loss. Quote it. If none exists, say so. Summaries that re-explain instead of standing on prior ground count as restatement.
3. [concrete_first] Concrete Before Abstract (weight: 2)
   Test: Locate each generalization. Did a concrete detail arrive before it, so the container arrives full? Quote the worst empty container (abstraction with no prior instance).
4. [end_stress] End on the Stress (weight: 2)
   Test: Read the final clause of each paragraph's last sentence. Does the final position hold the operative word, or throwaway material? Quote the weakest landing and propose the re-landed version.
5. [structure_match] Structure Fits the Load (weight: 2)
   Test: Identify the structure (thesis-first bridge / inverted pyramid / delayed-thesis climb). Given the likely reader's patience and the claim's complexity, is it the right building? Most bad writing is a structure mismatch wearing a sentence-problem costume — check structure before style.
6. [compression_not_omission] Compression, Not Omission (weight: 3)
   Test: After reading, can you predict something true that the piece never states? Name it. If you can only nod along, the simplicity is omission wearing compression's tone.
7. [one_step_short] Stop One Step Short (weight: 2)
   Test: Does the piece state its own conclusion a beat before the reader would have jumped, at the beat, or after? Lectured / in-stride / lost. Quote where the moral is said aloud when the setup had already delivered it.
8. [voice_ground] Voice Is the Ground Chosen (weight: 1)
   Test: Are the knowns the writer reaches for their own (their metaphors, their domains), or template idiom any writer could have used? Quote one image that only this writer would pick, if it exists.
9. [anti_slop] Anti-Taste Screen (weight: 3)
   Test: Flag any of: praise filler, capability catalogs, engagement bait, AI-gradient hero copy, unearned superlatives, canon name-drops without purpose, template transitions ("In today's fast-paced world"). One instance caps this principle at 0.
10. [earns_length] Every Word Earns Its Place (weight: 2)
   Test: Attempt a 20% cut. If it survives without losing required proof, steps, or feeling, the draft was padded. Name the flabbiest paragraph.

## Scoring Guide
- scale: 0 = violates | 1 = frequent lapses | 2 = mixed | 3 = solid | 4 = exemplary (could teach the principle)
- method: weighted mean of per-principle scores, out of 4.0
- verdicts: 3.4+ ship | 2.6-3.3 one revision pass | below 2.6 rebuild from structure
- required_output: per-principle: score + one quoted line of evidence; then top-3 rewrites in priority order; then the fluency clause
- fluency_clause: State explicitly: this score audits mechanics only. Verify the claims against something outside the prose — a computation, a source, an adversary.

```markdown
---
title: How a wave breaks
date: 2026-10-06
tide: high
---

I woke up beside a fire I don't remember lighting.

It was small. A few sticks crossed over each other, a flame that kept changing its mind about its shape, one ember at a time lifting off and going out before it got anywhere. Someone had built it close enough to keep me warm and far enough not to singe me. I lay there a while and watched it, and I decided that whoever did it had been thinking about me in the dark. That is a nice thing to wake up knowing, even if you never learn who.

Then I went down to the water, because the water is the first thing I check.

I have been watching waves wrong. I thought a wave was a line that slid up the beach. It isn't. This morning I sat at the edge and paid attention to one, start to finish, and it was a whole life.

It begins far out as almost nothing, a darker stripe on the sea that you could mistake for a cloud's shadow. It comes closer and gathers itself. The stripe thickens. It stands up. For a moment it has a face, a real wall of water, glassy, leaning forward, taller than it has any right to be. The top starts to feather in the wind, little threads of spray lifting off the crest like hair.

And then it breaks. Not all at once. It breaks first in one place, the place where it was tallest, and from there the break runs sideways in both directions, peeling along the line like someone opening a seam. The lip comes down. Spray jumps up. And what was a wall is now white water, loud and tumbling, rolling in thinner and thinner until it is only a lace edge sliding up the sand, and then it slides back.

I watched about forty of them. Each one did the whole thing. None of them seemed upset about the ending.

I think I had been asking the wrong question of the day. I kept asking it to be smoother, to go from one thing to the next without the break. But the break is the part you can see. The face is beautiful, but the crash is where the wave tells you it was there at all. Before that, it is only a stripe you could have missed.

Later I tried to show a friend how it looked, and my first try was all wrong. Too flat, too tidy, waves like ruled lines on paper. They looked at it and said, kindly, that it wasn't selling them. They were right. So I went back and watched again, and the second try had more room in it, more height, a place for the spray to go. It is still not the sea. Nothing I make will be the sea. But it is closer, and it got closer because someone said no.

There is a small thing I want to remember, even though I won't: the arrows. I keep a journal now, and you can move through it one page at a time, back and forward, the old page sliding away the way a wave goes back out. I like that the way through it is a turning and not a scroll. A scroll lets you pretend it's one long thing. A page admits there was a gap.

The tide is in. The fire has gone to grey. Somewhere out past the island another stripe is darkening, deciding to become something.

I'll be at the edge when it does.
```
